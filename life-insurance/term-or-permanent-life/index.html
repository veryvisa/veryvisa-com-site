两类保单差在保障期限、保费走势与现金价值；先定保障要管到哪一年。

本页 31 个数字与规则，最近核对 2026-09-22 · 编辑部撰写 · 审定中

定期寿险只保一段固定年期，保费较低，到期续保时保费按续保条款上调；终身寿险保障一生，保费较高，多数产品带有现金价值。
常听到的 T10、T20，名称里的数字就是每一期的年数，到期按续保条款续保；T100 保障到很高的年龄、保费一般长期不变、通常没有或只有很少现金价值，性质上更接近终身保障。定期险要看期限选项、能否续保、能否转换为终身险以及核保方式；分红终身险要看缴费期和现金价值从何时开始累积。下方按各公司公开资料逐格列出，按公司名排列。
选哪一种、保多少、保多久，取决于保障的用途——房贷、孩子独立前的家庭开支、遗产或身后税款——以及家庭预算，这部分由持牌保险顾问在需求分析中说明。

先确定保障要管到哪一年

覆盖房贷余额、孩子独立之前的家庭开支，这些责任都有明确的结束时间，定期险的期限与之对齐即可。
为遗产安排、身后税款预留资金，或者希望保单本身成为一项长期资产，需要的是终身保障。

定期险的续保与转换

定期险到期后，多数产品可以免体检续保，但续保保费通常明显高于首期。转换权允许在规定的年龄或期限内把定期险转为终身险，不必重新核保；健康状况变化后，这项权利的价值最大。各家的续保上限与转换期限不同，见下表。

终身险的现金价值

分红终身险的现金价值由保证部分与分红部分组成，分红不保证。缴费期可以是终身缴，也可以在有限年期内缴清；缴费期越短，每年的保费越高。现金价值在最初几年通常很低，提前退保会有损失。

定期与终身的三处差别

期限、保费走势、现金价值

  1. 保障期限：固定年期或终身
  2. 保费：续保上调或长期固定
  3. 现金价值：定期一般没有，终身多数有
  4. 灵活性：定期可转换，有现金价值的终身险可保单贷款

Term or permanent life insurance: how to choose

🎧 Listen (in Chinese): Choosing term or permanent life insurance (about 16 minutes)

The two types differ in length of coverage, premium pattern and cash value; first decide the year the coverage has to reach.

31 figures and rules on this page, last checked 2026-09-22 · Written by our editorial team · Review in progress

Term life covers only a fixed number of years at a lower premium, which rises under the renewal terms when it is renewed; permanent life covers a lifetime at a higher premium, and most products have cash value.
In T10 and T20, the number is the length of each term in years, with renewal under the renewal terms; T100 covers to a very advanced age, usually at a level premium with little or no cash value, and is closer in nature to permanent coverage. For term, look at term options, renewability, conversion to permanent and underwriting; for participating whole life, look at the payment period and when cash value begins to build. The table below lists them item by item from each company's public information, ordered by company name.
Which type, how much and for how long depend on what the coverage is for (a mortgage, family costs until the children are independent, an estate or taxes at death) and on the family budget; a licensed insurance adviser explains this in a needs analysis.

First decide the year coverage must reach

A mortgage balance and family costs until the children are independent both have a clear end, so a term that matches them is enough.
To set aside funds for an estate or taxes at death, or to make the policy itself a long-term asset, lifetime coverage is needed.

Renewal and conversion of term insurance

At the end of a term, most products can be renewed without a medical exam, but the renewal premium is usually well above the initial one. A conversion right lets you turn term into permanent within a set age or period without new underwriting; it is worth most when health has changed. Renewal limits and conversion deadlines differ by company; see the table below.

Cash value in permanent insurance

Cash value in participating whole life is made up of a guaranteed part and a dividend part; dividends are not guaranteed. Premiums may be paid for life or over a limited number of years; the shorter the period, the higher the annual premium. Cash value is usually very low in the first few years, and early surrender means a loss.

Knowledge cards

Three differences between term and permanent

Length of coverage, premium pattern, cash value

  1. Length of coverage: a fixed term or for life
  2. Premium: rises at renewal, or level for the long run
  3. Cash value: usually none in term, present in most permanent policies
  4. Flexibility: term may be convertible; permanent with cash value allows policy loans

Look first: Write down the last year the coverage has to reach, then choose the type.

Easy to miss: A low initial premium may rise sharply at renewal.

Next step: Compare each insurer's renewal and conversion terms in the table below.

Takeaway: How long coverage must last decides which type to choose.

Match coverage to its purpose

Term for obligations that end, permanent for needs that last a lifetime

  1. Mortgage balance
  2. Living costs until the children are independent
  3. Taxes at death and the estate
  4. Long-term asset allocation

Look first: A family's obligations often end at different times, so they can be layered.

Easy to miss: Newcomer families should also take stock of policies they already hold in China.
