重疾险在确诊符合合同定义的疾病后按约定给付现金；诊断名称只是起点，病理、严重程度、存活期与除外条件都可能影响结论。
癌症等待期与早期癌定义必须回到每份保单原文。下方完整列出已核对产品的主病、早期保障、多次赔付、退还保费与期限条款。
比较时不能只挑病种数量或一项附加保障；个人适合怎样的保障，由持牌保险顾问结合健康记录、现有保障与预算说明。

先读定义，再数病种

疾病名称旁的医学定义决定是否触发赔付。癌症要看组织学、侵袭程度与除外类型；心脏、脑血管与器官疾病也各有检查和严重程度条件。
病种数量适合用来确认覆盖范围，却不能单独表示保障更宽。定义、部分给付、复发安排与生存期需要一起读。

等待期与早期保障

等待期是保单生效后的一段特殊期间。期间出现症状、检查或诊断时，后果要按合同的通知与除外条款判断。
早期病保障可能按保额的一部分给付，并设最高金额、次数或后续主病保障的调整。癌症等待期的具体写法，表格里没有逐份列出，要回到每份保单原文核对。

附加保障怎样比较

多次赔付要看两次疾病之间是否要求不同病组、等待间隔或生存期；退还保费要看触发时点、退还范围与保单终止后果。
演示中的附加保障名称相近，合同条件却可能不同。投保前应索取正式说明文件，并把顾问说明与保单原文逐项对照。

  2. 检查与病理证据

附加保障核对表

多次赔付与退费条件分开读

  1. 早期病部分给付
  2. 不同病组要求
  3. 再次赔付间隔
  4. 退还保费时点
  5. 保单终止后果

先看：先确认主保障，再看附加项有没有改变核心责任。

下一步：在相同保额与条件下比较完整合同。

带走：附加项不能脱离主合同比较。

常见情形

体检发现原位病变。是否属于主病、早期病或不在保障内，要按该保单的定义核对。
家族有癌症史，最关心等待期。应同时看等待期内出现症状、接受检查与确诊各自怎样处理。
考虑带退还保费的附加保障。先确认退还条件会不会改变现金流与退保选择，再比较主保障本身。

深读

  • 「加拿大14家重疾险对比，病种差不多为何赔法不同」——讲病种数量相近时，定义与给付结构为何仍有差别。
  • 「确诊早期癌症，为什么重疾险未必全额赔付」——讲早期病与主病给付之间的区别。
  • 「重疾险确诊了为什么还被拒赔？七成栽在"定义"这三个字上」——讲理赔时怎样对照医学记录与合同定义。

Critical illness insurance: how insurers' definitions differ

The same illness name does not mean the same trigger, early-stage coverage or form of payment.

30 figures and rules on this page, last checked 2026-09-22 · Written by our editorial team · Review in progress

Critical illness insurance pays cash as agreed once an illness meeting the contract's definition is diagnosed. The name of the diagnosis is only a starting point; pathology, severity, the survival period and exclusions can all affect the outcome.
The waiting period for cancer and the definition of early-stage cancer must be read in each policy's own wording. The terms checked so far are listed in full below: main conditions, early-stage coverage, multiple claims, return of premium and term.
A comparison should not pick out only the number of illnesses or one rider. What coverage suits a person is explained by a licensed insurance adviser in light of health records, existing coverage and budget.

Read the definitions before counting illnesses

The medical definition next to the illness name decides whether a claim is triggered. Cancer turns on histology, invasion and excluded types; heart, cerebrovascular and organ conditions each have their own test and severity conditions.
The number of illnesses helps confirm the scope, but does not by itself mean wider coverage. Definitions, partial payments, recurrence and the survival period need to be read together.

Waiting period and early-stage coverage

A waiting period is a special period after the policy takes effect. If symptoms, tests or a diagnosis occur within it, the consequences follow the contract's notice and exclusion clauses.
Early-stage coverage may pay part of the coverage amount, with a maximum, a limited number of payments or an adjustment to later main coverage. The exact wording on the cancer waiting period is not listed policy by policy in the table; check each policy's own wording.

How to compare riders

For multiple claims, see whether the two illnesses must be in different groups and whether a waiting interval or survival period applies; for return of premium, see when it is triggered, how much is returned and what happens to the policy.
Riders may have similar names in an illustration but different contract terms. Before applying, ask for the official documents and compare the adviser's explanation with the policy wording, point by point.

Knowledge cards

Knowledge cards

The layers in an illness definition

Beyond the name of the illness lie medical and contract thresholds

  1. Name of the diagnosis
  2. Test and pathology evidence
  3. Severity
  4. Waiting and survival conditions
  5. Exclusions and notice

Look first: Start by finding the full definition in the actual policy that matches the diagnosis.

Easy to miss: The same illness name may be split into full-benefit, early-stage and excluded cases.

Next step: Match the key wording in the doctor's records to the definition, point by point.

Takeaway: A claim is paid on the definition, not just on the name of the illness.

Knowledge cards

Checklist for riders

Read multiple-claim and return-of-premium conditions separately

  1. Partial payment for early-stage illness
  2. Requirement of a different illness group
  3. Interval before another claim
  4. When premiums are returned
  5. Consequences when the policy ends

Look first: Confirm the base coverage first, then see whether a rider changes the core obligation.

Easy to miss: When premiums are returned, the policy may end at the same time.

Next step: Compare full contracts with the same coverage amount and conditions.

Takeaway: A rider cannot be compared apart from the main contract.

Common situations

A check-up finds an in-situ lesion. Whether it is a main condition, an early-stage one or not covered has to be checked against that policy's definition.
There is a family history of cancer and the waiting period is the main concern. Look together at how symptoms, tests and a diagnosis within the waiting period are each handled.
You are considering a return of premium rider. First see whether the return conditions would change cash flow and surrender choices, then compare the main coverage itself.
