离境前的核对顺序

  1. 确定非居民日期：配偶与孩子是否同时离开，到国内后从哪一天成为当地的税务居民。
  2. 列出全部财产：哪些属于视同处置的范围，哪些在例外清单里。加拿大的自住房、RRSP、TFSA 不按离境视同处置。
  3. 计算视同处置的增值：按离境当天的公允市值减去成本。股票、基金、加拿大境外的房产都在其中。

常见情形

夫妻一方先回国工作，另一方带孩子留在温哥华读书。家人仍在加拿大时，先回国的一方通常不会因此成为非居民，离境日期要等全家的安排确定后再判断。
离境前持有加拿大的股票账户与国内的房产。两者都在视同处置范围内，按离境当天的市值计算增值；保留在加拿大的房子不在其中，日后出售时按非居民卖房的规则清税。
RRSP 里有一笔积蓄，打算回国后再取。RRSP 不随离境视同处置，可以留在账户里继续增长；回国后提取时按非居民规则预扣税款，国内是否征税另行了解。

Accounts outside departure tax

RRSPs, TFSAs, RRIFs and pension plans are not deemed disposed of on departure. What happens once they stay in Canada (holding on, non-resident withholding on withdrawals, whether China taxes the income) has rules of its own; see the pages on registered accounts and pensions.

Knowledge cards

The order of steps in a departure return

Once the date is fixed, every later step has a basis

  1. Establish the date you became a non-resident
  2. Separate property deemed disposed of from the exceptions
  3. Check whether total value means a list must be filed
  4. Decide whether to pay that year or defer

Look first: The dates your spouse and children leave often matter more than the date on your own ticket.

Easy to miss: Stocks and funds you have not sold also have their gains worked out at the price on the day you leave.

Next step: If you need to defer the tax, see the next card.

Takeaway: Fix the date first; then the return has a basis.

Knowledge cards

Departure tax can be deferred

Consider electing to defer together with the security threshold

  1. Make the election in the return for the year of departure
  2. Security is required if the tax exceeds the security threshold
  3. Settle when the property is actually sold
  4. Deferral is not forgiveness

Look first: Estimate the federal tax from the deemed disposition and see which side of the threshold it falls on.

Easy to miss: You must elect to defer; without an election, the tax is due for that year.

Next step: An adviser works out the form and amount of security with the CRA, based on the assets.

Takeaway: Deferring the tax is a choice, and you have to ask for it.

Common situations

One spouse goes back to work in China first while the other stays in Vancouver with the children at school. While the family remains in Canada, the spouse who leaves first usually does not become a non-resident for that reason; the departure date is judged once the whole family's plans are settled.
Before leaving, you hold a Canadian stock account and property in China. Both fall under the deemed disposition, with gains worked out at market value on the day of departure; a home kept in Canada does not, and a later sale follows the clearance rules for non-residents selling real estate.
You have savings in an RRSP and plan to withdraw them after returning to China. The RRSP is not deemed disposed of on departure and can keep growing in the account; withdrawals after you leave have tax withheld under non-resident rules, and whether China taxes them is a separate question.
