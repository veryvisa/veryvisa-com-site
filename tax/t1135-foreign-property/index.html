哪些算指定财产

国内的银行存款、理财产品、股票与基金账户、借给境外个人或机构、由对方欠您的款项、境外公司的股份，以及用来出租的房产，一般都属于境外指定财产。
主要供本人或亲属个人使用的财产不算，包括主要用于自住或度假的房子，以及珠宝、字画、邮票等收藏品；是否「主要」自用，CRA 按实际使用情况个案判断。
出租的房子要分情况：以盈利为目的出租的，属于指定财产；只是为了收回物业开支、并不期望盈利的短期出租，仍按个人使用看待。

常见情形

落地第二年，国内有本人名下与父母名下的存款。只有本人名下、或本人实际拥有份额的存款计入；成为居民的当年不用报，第二年起按全年最高时的成本判断。
国内有一套从前自住的房子，现在空置，回国时偶尔居住。主要供个人使用的房产不计入；如果改为长期出租收租，就要按指定财产重新判断。
夫妻共同持有国内的证券账户。按各自的份额分别计算成本，两人各自判断是否需要申报。

What counts as specified property

Bank deposits, wealth management products, stock and fund accounts in China, money owed to you by a person or institution outside Canada, shares of foreign companies, and real estate rented out are generally specified foreign property.
Property mainly for the personal use of you or your relatives does not count, including a home mainly lived in or used for vacations, and collections such as jewellery, paintings and stamps; whether use is “mainly” personal is judged by the CRA case by case on actual use.
A rented home depends on the case: rented for profit, it is specified property; a short rental only to recover property expenses, without expecting profit, is still treated as personal use.

How cost is added up

Cost is generally the price paid plus related expenses, converted into Canadian dollars. Property already held before becoming a resident follows another rule: certain property held on immigrating is deemed to be disposed of and reacquired at fair market value on the day of entry

Knowledge cards

Four steps to see whether you must file

Sort first, then add up the cost

  1. List all assets in China
  2. Remove the home you live in and personal-use property
  3. Add up the cost of what remains
  4. See whether the highest point of the year passed the threshold

Look first: The second step settles the answer for most families: be clear whether a home is mainly for personal use.

Easy to miss: The threshold is measured on the price paid; growth in the account does not change that number.

Next step: If you are over the threshold, choose the method on the next card.

Takeaway: Separate personal use from investment, then add up cost.

Knowledge cards

Simplified and detailed reporting

Choose by the band the year's cost falls in

  1. Year you become a resident: not filed
  2. Year's cost below the simplified limit: report by category
  3. At the simplified limit: list each property
  4. Year of departure: report the resident period only

Look first: The band your highest cost of the year falls in decides which method to use.

Easy to miss: The first-year exemption covers only the year you become a resident; from the second year on, the usual test applies.

Next step: If past years were not filed, organize the cost records first, then look at filing late.

Takeaway: How to fill in the form depends on the highest cost during the year.

Common situations

It is the second year after landing, and there are deposits in China in your name and in your parents' names. Only deposits in your name, or the share you actually own, count; nothing is reported for the year you became a resident, and from the second year the test is the highest cost during the year.
A home you used to live in in China is now empty, and you stay there on visits. Property mainly for personal use does not count; if it becomes a long-term rental, it must be judged again as specified property.
Spouses jointly hold a securities account in China. Work out the cost by each one's share, and each decides separately whether to report.
