一年有半年在国内，还算加拿大税务居民吗

Canadian tax residency when you spend half the year in China

Look at residential ties first, then days of presence; where both countries' rules apply, follow the treaty order.

7 figures and rules on this page, last checked 2026-10-01 · Written by our editorial team · Review in progress

Whether you are a tax resident of Canada depends first on your residential ties with Canada. If your home is in Canada, or your spouse or dependants live here, the factual resident rules usually have to be checked further.
183 days
Source and check date

CRA: official source

 This rule comes into play when there are no significant residential ties. Part of a day also counts: For the 183-day deemed resident test, the CRA counts any part of a day as a full day.

Look first: Look at the family home and the closest ties of daily life first.

The order for checking two-country residency

If one step settles it, compare no further

  1. Permanent home
  2. Centre of vital interests
  3. Habitual abode
  4. Nationality
  5. Agreement between the competent authorities

Look first: Confirm whether both countries treat you as a resident.

Easy to miss: Family and economic ties need to be read together.

Next step: If it is still unclear, bring full documents to a professional.

Takeaway: The treaty deals with cases where both sets of rules point to you.

Common situations

Parents spend long periods in China each year while keeping a home in Vancouver; significant residential ties must still be checked first.
When spouses live in two places, both family and economic ties belong in the overall facts.
When the whole family leaves Canada and disposes of the main home, also look at the departure date and the related filings.
