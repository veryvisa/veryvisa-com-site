漏报会丢掉免税或被罚

  1. 出售当年申报
  2. 填写指定表
  3. 漏报补做
  4. 按月计罚

先看：看清楚报税软件里卖房那一栏有没有勾。

常见情形

买房后一直自住，换房时卖掉旧房。增值通常全部免税，但卖出那年仍要申报出售并填指定表，不报就可能失去免税。
自住几年后搬去别的城市工作，把房子出租，几年后再卖。搬走那年若做了选择，出租的头几年仍可指定为主要住所；没做的，搬走那天按市值视同卖出一次。
夫妻一人名下一套自住房、一人名下一套度假屋。同一年只能有一套算主要住所，先卖的那套要决定用掉多少年。

Capital gains tax when you sell your home

🎧 Listen (in Chinese): The principal residence exemption and reporting a sale (about 17 minutes)

Most of the gain on the home you live in can be tax-free. Whether it is, and how much, depends on how many years you lived there, whether it was rented, and whether you reported as required in the year of sale.

One family can designate only one home a year

The rule is direct: a family can designate only one principal residence for each tax year
Source and check date

CRA: official source

only one property per family unit can be designated as a principal residence

Effective: not stated · Checked: 2026-11-04 · ✅ primary source · Next review: 2027-05-04

. Family here means you, your spouse or common-law partner (unless living apart under a separation all year), and unmarried children under eighteen. If each spouse has a home in their own name, only one of them can be the principal residence for the same year.

Families generally compare the average yearly gain of the two homes and give the years first to the one that gained more per year. It is worth working this out before selling the first home.

Homes held for less than a year

Even the home you live in is treated as a business if held too briefly: a gain on a home held for fewer than 365 consecutive days is treated as business income, not a capital gain, except for certain life events

Knowledge cards

The principal residence exemption is counted in years

The more years lived in, the larger the share exempt

  2. Tax years designated
  3. The extra year
  4. The remaining taxable part

Next step: Make a table of where you lived each year and whether you were a resident.

Takeaway: Live in it throughout and report on time, and the whole gain is usually tax-free.

You must report in the year you sell

Missing the report can lose the exemption or bring a penalty

  1. Report in the year of sale
  2. Complete the designation form
  3. Make up a missed report
  4. Penalty counted by the month

Look first: Check whether the home-sale box in your tax software is ticked.

Easy to miss: Report even if you lived there throughout; leaving it out does not make the sale disappear.

Next step: If you find you missed it, ask the CRA to change that year's return as soon as possible.

Takeaway: Reporting costs almost nothing; the cost of missing it builds up month by month.

Common situations

You lived in the home from purchase and sell it when moving. The gain is usually fully exempt, but the sale must still be reported and the designation form completed for that year; without reporting, the exemption may be lost.
After living in the home for several years you move to another city for work, rent it out and sell it years later. If the election was made in the year you moved, the first years of rental can still be designated as principal residence; if not, the home is deemed sold once at market value on the day you moved.
One spouse owns the home you live in and the other owns a vacation home. Only one of them can be the principal residence for a given year; whichever sells first, decide how many years to use.
